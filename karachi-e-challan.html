Karachi E-Challans: Why Enforcement Must Continue

By Saeed Rasheed

Letter to the Editor

Subject: E-Challan System Must Be Continuous to Maintain Traffic Discipline

Sir,

Through the columns of your esteemed newspaper, I wish to highlight the importance of consistency in the implementation of the e-challan system in Karachi.

The introduction of the faceless e-challan system during October and November was a commendable initiative by law-enforcement agencies. According to publicly available information, fines amounting to nearly Rs. 1 billion were generated during this period, which clearly reflects both the scale of violations and the effectiveness of the system.

It is acknowledged that no new system is free from errors. There were instances of incorrect or duplicate challans, which certainly need rectification. However, such shortcomings should be addressed side by side through technical improvements and grievance redressal mechanisms, rather than by slowing down or silencing the entire system.

More importantly, the system had a visible deterrent effect. Citizens began following traffic rules more seriouslyespecially regarding helmet usage, traffic signals, seat belts, lane discipline, and avoidance of wrong-way driving. For the first time in years, there was a noticeable improvement in road behaviour, proving that enforcement, when applied impartially, does work.

Unfortunately, the sudden silence and apparent suspension of the e-challan drivehave sent the wrong message to the public. People have now started if the system has failed or been abandoned. This perception is damaging, not only to traffic discipline but also to the credibility of law-enforcement institutions.

A basic principle of law enforcement is continuity. Either a system should not be introduced at all, or once introduced, it must not be stopped. Otherwise, it loses its credibility and deterrent value.

Law enforcement must understand that selective or temporary enforcement encourages violations, while sustained enforcement builds discipline. Karachi desperately needs a consistent traffic management system, not short-term campaigns.

I urge the concerned authorities to restore and continuously enforce the e-challan system, ensuring transparency, fairness, and uninterrupted operation. Only then can traffic discipline become a permanent feature rather than a temporary reaction.
