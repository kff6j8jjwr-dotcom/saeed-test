Tirah Valley Displacement: A Cost of Provincial Negligence

By Saeed Rasheed

Tirah Valley Displacement: History, Facts, and the Cost of Provincial Negligence

The people of Tirah Valley, located in the former FATA region of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, have a long-documented history of seasonal displacement. Every year, with the onset of extreme winter, families voluntarily move from higher altitudes to relatively safer and warmer areas. This is neither new nor unusual; it is a survival practice shaped by geography and climate, not by military necessity.

However, the current round of displacement has become controversial due to conflicting political narratives and administrative failures.

Historical Context

Traditionally, Tirah residents migrate during harsh winters without large-scale state intervention. These movements are voluntary, predictable, and manageable when planned properly. In the past, limited assistance and communitylevel coordination were sufficient.

Recent Developments

In the present case, multiple jirgas were conducted by the provincial government under the leadership of Ali Amin Gandapur, and other provincial authority with federal administration, aimed at ensuring what was described as a “smooth and voluntary displacement” during the winter season.

According to provincial statements:

Displacement was seasonal and voluntary

A massive relief package of Rs 4 billion was sanctioned

The process was framed as a humanitarian measure, not a security operation

Emerging Conflict

Contrary to these claims, strong resistance emerged from local communities and civil society. The central concern was that:

The displacement was allegedly linked to pressure for a largescale security operation

Such an operation would require mass evacuation, unlike intelligencebased operations which do not displace civilians

Residents feared being used as collateral in a broader security strategy

The federal government categorically denied requesting any largescale military operation in Tirah, clarifying that:

No full-scale operation was approved

Winter conditions make such operations impractical

There was no directive for mass displacement

This contradiction exposed a serious governance gap between federal policy and provincial execution.

Jirga Minutes & Administrative Timeline

A review of jirga minutes and provincial notifications shows:

No formal emergency declaration was issued initially

Relief mechanisms were announced after displacement had already begun

Shelter, food, and transport arrangements lagged population movement

This raises a critical question:If displacement was voluntary and seasonal, why was emergency funding required on such an extraordinary scale?

Provincial Negligence

The humanitarian situation deteriorated due to:

Delayed planning

Lack of transparent relief distribution

Absence of winter-specific infrastructure

Poor coordination between civil administration and local representatives

Adding to the controversy, the Chief Minister remained politically active, participating in street protests in Lahore and Karachi, while Tirah residents faced exposure, hunger, and uncertainty.

Conclusion

The Tirah crisis is not a security failure but a governance failure. Seasonal migration could have been managed with dignity and minimal suffering. Instead, administrative confusion, political messaging, and delayed action turned a predictable situation into a humanitarian emergency.

The people of Tirah did not demand confrontation — they demanded preparation.
