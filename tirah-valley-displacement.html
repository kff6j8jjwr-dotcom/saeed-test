Tirah Valley Displacement: A Cost of Provincial Negligence

By Saeed Rasheed

Families in Tirah Valley have long moved seasonally from higher altitudes during severe winters. This predictable survival practice need not become a humanitarian crisis when it is planned with dignity and local coordination.

Conflicting Narratives

The latest displacement became controversial amid claims of voluntary seasonal movement, a major relief package, and concerns that residents were being prepared for a large-scale security operation. Federal authorities denied any directive for mass displacement, exposing a governance gap between policy and execution.

A Failure of Preparation

Jirga records and notifications indicate that relief, shelter, food, and transport arrangements lagged behind population movement. Delayed planning, poor coordination, and a lack of transparent relief distribution made a predictable winter challenge far worse.

The Tirah crisis is presented as a governance failure, not simply a security failure. Residents did not demand confrontation; they demanded preparation.
