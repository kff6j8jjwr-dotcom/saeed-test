Pakistan’s Economic Myth: Debt, Taxes & Structural Reform

By Saeed Rasheed

Pakistan’s public debate often treats bad governance and corruption as the sole explanation for economic distress. Both matter, but fiscal data suggests a deeper structural problem: debt servicing, a narrow tax base, cash dependence, and a revenue-sharing structure that constrains the federal government.

The Debt Trap

Using FY 2024–25 figures cited in the article, revenue was PKR 12.2 trillion against expenditure of PKR 18.7 trillion, with debt servicing consuming the largest share. New borrowing is then used to fill the gap, raising future interest obligations and squeezing development.

What Must Change

The argument is for greater documentation, stronger direct taxation, a broader tax base, and political courage to reform the cash economy. Without structural change, the cycle can repeat regardless of who governs.
