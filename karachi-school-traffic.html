Karachi’s School-Hour Traffic Crisis

By Saeed Rasheed

Karachi Traffic Nonsense: A Failure of Regulation and Enforcement

Karachi’s traffic congestion during school hours has crossed the threshold from inconvenience to systemic administrative failure. Every weekday, particularly between 8:00 a.m.in morning and 2:00–2:30 p.m., major arteries of the city are effectively blocked due to unregulated pickanddrop activities of large schools, colleges, and universities.

This daily paralysis is not an act of fate; it is the direct consequence of regulatory negligence. Roads in front of prominent educational institutions routinely remain congested for up to one hour, causing severe hardship to citizens. Employees are delayed, businesses suffer losses, and, most critically, ambulances and emergency vehicles are obstructed, posing a clear threat to the constitutional right to life.

Under established principles of public nuisance law, no private or semi-private institution has the right to obstruct public roads or interfere with free movement. The continued allowance of roadside pickand drop activities constitutes a failure of civic duty by both the institutions and the concerned authorities.

In developed jurisdictions such as the United States and the United Kingdom, traffic management during school hours is a mandatory institutional obligation, enforced through licensing conditions. Even for mosque administrations arrange traffic discipline during Friday prayers and payment to traffic Police staff during these hours paid by Mosque administration. The question then arises: why are educational institutions allowed to operate without similar obligations?

Economic, Environmental, and Human Costs

The cost of this negligence is immense:

Millions of rupees worth of fuel are wasted daily, aggravating the national energy crisis.

Thousands of productive work hours are lost, impacting economic efficiency.

Increased congestion leads to higher accident rates, pollution, and public frustration.

Legal and Administrative Remedies

Immediate corrective measures are required:

No No-Objection Certificate (NOC) should be issued or renewed for any educational institution unless it provides:

A large-scale Audit will be conducted, and notices will be issued to institutes for compliance of:

An internal transit route for pick-and-drop

Adequate playground space as per zoning laws

Designated parking for teachers and visitors

All Fire and safety procedure and equipment’s, training, rapid avocation procedure are available/ assemblies point and regular fire drill

NO PARKING law strictly applied outside school premises

Traffic management must be arranged and funded by the institution, not imposed on the public or overburdened traffic police.

Institutions operating on main roads without internal circulation facilities must be treated as violators of municipal and traffic regulations.

Authorities must recognize such congestion as a form of public nuisance, attracting penalties, fines, and potential closure for repeated violations.

Education is a public good, but it cannot be allowed to operate at the expense of public rights. The freedom of one institution ends where the rights of thousands of citizens begin.

Karachi urgently needs rule-based urban governance, not selective enforcement. If laws exist only on paper and NOCs are issued without compliance, traffic chaos will remain the city’s daily punishment.

The solution lies not in advisories, but in strict enforcement, accountability, and legal compliance.

KARACHI
