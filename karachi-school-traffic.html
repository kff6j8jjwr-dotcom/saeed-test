Karachi’s School-Hour Traffic Crisis

By Saeed Rasheed · Karachi

Karachi’s congestion during school hours has become a systemic administrative failure. Unregulated pick-and-drop activity outside major schools, colleges, and universities blocks key roads, delays workers, damages businesses, wastes fuel, and obstructs ambulances and emergency vehicles.

Public Rights and Institutional Duty

No institution should be allowed to obstruct public roads or interfere with free movement. In well-regulated cities, school traffic management is an institutional obligation, enforced through licensing conditions and internal circulation plans.

Required Enforcement

NOCs should require internal pick-and-drop routes, adequate parking and playground space, safety equipment and drills, and strict no-parking enforcement outside premises. Institutions must arrange and fund traffic management rather than shifting the cost to the public and traffic police.

Education is a public good, but it cannot operate at the expense of public rights. Karachi needs rule-based enforcement, accountability, and legal compliance.
