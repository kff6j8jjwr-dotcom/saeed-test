Fuel Prices vs Politics: Pakistan’s Economic Reality

By Saeed Rasheed

Fuel-price increases often produce strong public reaction, yet their direct household impact should be judged through actual usage and spending data. Motorcycles are Pakistan’s most common form of affordable transport, and an illustrative Rs 58-per-litre increase adds about Rs 1,044 per month to a motorcycle using 18 litres—approximately Rs 17 per day per rider where two people share it.

Beyond the Headline

Inflation, food, rent, electricity, education, and health care are broader household pressures. Better public transport, income growth, fuel efficiency, entrepreneurship, and wage adjustments are more durable responses than political messaging alone.

Net Metering

The article also considers the claim that solar net metering burdens poor consumers. With an estimated 250,000 users among roughly 42 million electricity consumers, net-metering users represent less than one percent of the base. Economic discussion should be grounded in proportionate evidence and long-term policy.
