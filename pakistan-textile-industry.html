Pakistan’s Textile Export Decline: The Cost of Inaction

By Saeed Rasheed

Pakistan’s textile sector has long been the backbone of exports, employment, and industry. Yet, despite cotton, labour, and experience, it is losing global apparel market share while competitors such as Bangladesh, India, and China advance through scale, technology, and value addition.

The Competitive Gap

Pakistan’s costs are weakened by low productivity, outdated machinery, high factory overheads, weak compliance systems, and delayed tax refunds. Energy is a challenge, but it is not the principal structural failure. Solar adoption, automation, and efficiency could materially reduce overheads.

The Real Causes

The deeper issues are limited innovation, insufficient investment in CAD/CAM and smart manufacturing, weak branding, and a tendency to extract profits rather than reinvest in modernisation. Competitors have built design capability, reliable compliance, and value-added garment production.

A Route Back

Pakistan can regain competitiveness through technology, solar financing, professional management, product design, and reinvestment. The country still has cotton, labour, and market access; what it needs is investment discipline and the courage to change.
