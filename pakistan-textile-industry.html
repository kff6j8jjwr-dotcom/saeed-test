Pakistan’s Textile Export Decline: The Cost of Inaction

By Saeed Rasheed

Downfall of Textile Export Industry in Pakistan: Structural Failures, Missed Innovation, and the Cost of Inaction

The textile sector has historically been the backbone of Pakistan’s exports, employment, and industrial base. Yet despite having cotton, Labor, and decades of experience, Pakistan continues to lose market share in global apparel exports especially in basic items such as T-shirts while regional competitors move ahead with speed, scale, and strategy.

Global Apparel Export Comparison (Approximate Annual USD Value)

China: USD 180–190 billion

India: USD 40–45 billion

Bangladesh: USD 47–50 billion

Sri Lanka: USD 5–6 billion

Taiwan: USD 12–14 billion

Pakistan: USD 16–18 billion

Despite similar starting points decades ago, Pakistan now lags far behind Bangladesh and India in value-added garments.

The T-Shirt Example: Where Pakistan Loses

A basic cotton T-shirt is one of the most exported apparel items globally. European and US buyers typically offer USD 2.5–4.0 per piece depending on quality, compliance, and volume.

2. Energy Crisis and Mismanagement

Unstable electricity, expensive gas, and policy uncertainty make planning impossible. Factories operate below capacity or shut down intermittently.

Critical Question:Why is the textile sector not aggressively shifting to solar energy, which is:

A one-time capital investment

Independent of government tariffs

Already proven viable in India, Bangladesh, and Vietnam

The absence of structured solar financing and incentives reflects policy paralysis.

Factory Overhead (FOH):

*Pakistan:19%

*Region countries average:13%

After Soar Energy:

Projected PAKISTAN FOH 12%

3. Lack of Innovation and Technology

Pakistan largely remains stuck in conventional production:

Low automation

Poor design capability

Minimal R&D

Little focus on technical textiles or fashion innovation

Competitors invest heavily in:

CAD/CAM design

Smart manufacturing

Value-added apparel instead of raw or semi-finished goods

4. Weak Compliance and Branding

Global buyers now demand:

Environmental compliance

Labor certifications

Traceability

Pakistan reacts late, while Bangladesh and Sri Lanka market compliance as a selling point.

5. Taxation and Refund Bottlenecks

Export-oriented industries face:

Delayed sales tax refunds

Arbitrary audits

Policy reversals

This converts exporters into involuntary financiers of the state.

Why Others Succeeded

Bangladesh focused on garments only, simplified policies, cheap financing, and scale.

India invested in technology parks, solar adoption, and design.

China moved up the value chain early, leaving low-end products once margins shrank.

MYTH VS REALITY

❌ MYTH 1: Energy Cost Is the Main Reason

Reality:Even with higher FOH, the difference is only 6–8%.This gap can be neutralized by solar, automation, and efficiency.

➡️ Energy is a manageable variable, not a structural failure.

❌ MYTH 2: High Taxes Destroy Competitiveness

Reality:Export-oriented textiles are zero-rated in most cases.The real issue is refunding delay, not tax rate.

➡️ Delays hurt cash flow, not cost structure.

THE REAL REASONS FOR DOWNFALL (DATA-SUPPORTED)

✅ 1. Lack of Innovation

Same designs for decades

No branding, no fashion cycles

Competing only on price, not value

Bangladesh sells a T-shirt.China sells a designed product.Pakistan sells fabric stitched into hope.

FINAL CONCLUSION (STRONG & CLEAR)

Pakistan’s textile export decline is not primarily caused by energy cost or tax rate.The data proves that the real failure lies in:

Lack of innovation

Outdated technology

Poor reinvestment culture

Absence of branding and value addition

Energy and taxes are convenient excuses, not root causes.

Nations that invest, innovate, and adapt survive.Nations that complain fall behind.

Pakistan still has cotton, labour, and markets.What it lacks is vision, courage to change, and investment discipline.
