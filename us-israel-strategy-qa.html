US–Israel Strategy: Questions & Answers

With Yasir · By Saeed Rasheed

Was the U.S. pulled into the Israel war without planning?

This is largely a media perception. Evidence suggests long-term planning of more than five years. The 2025 escalation appears to be a trigger phase rather than a surprise, with the objective of reducing Iran’s regional influence.

Are Iran and its proxy groups still a major threat?

Leadership of groups such as Hamas and Hezbollah has been significantly weakened, and command and coordination structures have been disrupted. The proxy network is less effective than before, though the threat has not been eliminated.

Has Iran’s nuclear and missile capability been destroyed?

Key nuclear-related facilities have been targeted and senior scientists and planners reportedly removed. Missile capability is reduced but still exists, while Iran appears to be in a defensive posture.

Is the U.S. withdrawing from the Middle East?

There is a gradual reduction of ground troops and a strategic shift toward intelligence support, advanced weapons supply, and naval presence: less direct involvement, more strategic control.

Can Israel defend itself independently?

Israel has demonstrated strong multi-layered defence systems and advanced capabilities in air defence, intelligence, and cyber operations. The message is a high level of self-reliance.

Is the Strait of Hormuz really under threat?

Historically it has never been fully closed, and global naval forces can respond quickly. The threat is real but is often exaggerated; the future may involve international monitoring or control.

Are drones economically exhausting advanced defence systems?

Most drones are intercepted using machine guns, anti-aircraft systems, and emerging laser technologies. Expensive missiles are reserved for high-value threats, making the “cheap drone versus expensive defence” narrative overstated.

How important is oil?

Energy security remains central: stable supply chains, reduced dependence on vulnerable routes, and influence over energy regions are strategically important.

What role will regional countries play?

The direction is toward self-reliant regional security. Countries are expected to strengthen their defence systems and form strategic partnerships, while the U.S. role becomes supportive rather than direct.

Is this global peace or power restructuring?

The strategy is presented as a long-term stability approach focused on reducing proxy conflicts and restructuring security responsibilities. In reality, it likely includes both stability goals and power interests.

Final question: war or planned transformation?

It appears to be more than just a war: a phased approach to weakening proxy networks, reducing strategic threats, and shifting the regional power balance. The outcome is still evolving. Further questions include the future of NATO, Russia’s future interests, and the consequences of the Ukraine war for Russia.
