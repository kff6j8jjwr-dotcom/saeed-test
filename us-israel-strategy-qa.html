US–Israel Strategy: Questions & Answers

By Saeed Rasheed

Yasir Q&A Session:

US–Israel Strategy (2025–2027)

Q1: Has the U.S. been pulled into the Israel war without planning?

Answer:

This is largely a media perception

Evidence suggests long-term planning (5+ years)

The 2025 escalation appears to be a trigger phase, not a surprise

Objective: Reduce Iran’s regional influence

Q2: Are Iran and its proxy groups still a major threat?

Answer:

Leadership of groups like Hamas and Hezbollah has been significantly weakened

Command and coordination structures disrupted

Proxy network is less effective than before

However, the threat is not eliminated

Q3: Has Iran’s nuclear and missile capability been destroyed?

Answer:

Key nuclear-related facilities have been targeted

Senior scientists and planners reportedly removed

Missile capability is reduced but still exists

Iran appears to be in a defensive posture

Q4: Is the U.S. withdrawing from the Middle East?

Answer:

Gradual reduction of ground troops

Strategic shift toward:

Intelligence support

Advanced weapons supply

Naval presence

Approach: “Less direct involvement, more strategic control”

Q5: Can Israel now defend itself independently?

Answer:

Israel has demonstrated strong multi-layered defence systems

Advanced capabilities in:

Air defence

Intelligence

Cyber operations

Message: High level of self-reliance

Q6: Is the Strait of Hormuz really under threat?

Answer:

Historically, it has never been fully closed

Global naval forces can respond quickly

Future may involve:

International monitoring or control

The threat is real but often exaggerated

Q7: Are drones economically exhausting advanced defence systems?

Answer:

Most drones are intercepted using:

Machine guns

Anti-aircraft systems

Emerging laser technologies

Expensive missiles are used only for high-value threats

The “cheap drone vs expensive defence” narrative is overstated

Q8: How important is oil in this strategy?

Answer:

Energy security remains a key factor

Objectives include:

Stable supply chains

Reduced dependence on vulnerable routes

Influence over energy regions is strategically important

Q9: What role will regional countries play?

Answer:

Shift toward self-reliant regional security

Countries expected to:

Strengthen their own defence systems

Form strategic partnerships

U.S. role becomes supportive rather than direct

Q10: Is this a move toward global peace or power restructuring?

Answer:

Presented as a long-term stability strategy

Focus on:

Reducing proxy conflicts

Restructuring security responsibilities

Reality likely includes both stability goals and power interests

Q11: Final question — is this a war or a planned transformation?

Answer:

It appears to be more than just a war

A phased approach:

Weakening proxy networks

Reducing strategic threats

Shifting regional power balance

The outcome is still evolving
