Pakistan’s Budget Trap: A Case for Structural Solutions

By Saeed Rasheed

THE BIG MYTH IN PAKISTAN:

“BAD GOVERNANCE AND CORRUPTION ARE THE MAIN ECONOMIC PROBLEMS”

Why the numbers tell a different story

In Pakistan’s public discourse, a single explanation dominates every economic debate:bad governance, federal extravagance, and corruption.

This belief is emotionally satisfying but economically incomplete.

When we examine the actual fiscal data, a different and far more uncomfortable truth emerges.

WHAT PEOPLE BELIEVE

Most citizens assume:

Federal expenses are out of control

Corruption alone is draining the economy

If governance improves, the crisis will end

This narrative is repeated on talk shows, drawing rooms, and social media without looking at numbers.

An economic reality check

The **Pakistan Government’s fiscal story for FY 2024–2025 exposes a dangerous contradiction:revenues have increased, yet debt has expanded further — pushing the country deeper into a cycle that cannot be resolved through cosmetic adjustments.

This is not a revenue problem alone.It is a structural failure.

1. Federal Expenses Are Not the Main Drain

Federal operational spending is a small fraction of total expenditure

Even eliminating all federal perks would not close the deficit

Cutting federal expenses is like removing a bandage from a broken bone — symbolic, not curative.

2. Corruption Is Costly — But Not the Core Deficit Driver

Corruption damages trust and efficiency, but:

It does not consume 70% of revenue

It does not automatically create PKR 6–7 trillion annual deficits

The biggest expense is not theft — it is interest.

3. Debt Servicing Is the Real Black Hole

Over half of federal revenue goes to debt repayment

Every new loan increase next year’s burden

Development spending is sacrificed to pay yesterday’s debts

This is mathematical, not moral.

4. NFC Structure Locks the Centre

A large portion of revenue is transferred to provinces before federal obligations are paid.This leaves the federal government borrowing even to run itself.

This is a design issue, not a governance failure.

5. Pakistan Has a Revenue Structure Problem

Extremely narrow tax base

Heavy reliance on indirect taxes

Massive cash economy

Millions earn but remain undocumented

No government can survive this structure — honest or corrupt.

THE REAL ECONOMIC PROBLEM: STRUCTURE, NOT MORALS

Pakistan’s crisis is driven by:

Low documentation

Weak direct taxation

Debt rollover dependency

Cash-based economy

Even a perfectly honest government would still face deficits under this framework.

WHY THE MYTH PERSISTS

Because blaming:

Politicians

Bureaucrats

Corruption

Is easier than confronting structural reform.

Real reform requires:

Universal documentation

Tax inclusion of salaried and self-employed

End of cash dominance

Political courage

THE UNCOMFORTABLE CONCLUSION

Pakistan is not collapsing because the government is wasteful.Pakistan is collapsing because too few people pay tax, too much money is borrowed, and old loans eat new income.

Until the structure changes, the crisis will repeat —regardless of who governs.

Revenue

Despite nominal growth, real purchasing power remains weak due to inflation and debt servicing pressure.

Major Expenditure Heads

1. NFC (National Finance Commission) Share

A significant portion of federal revenue is transferred to provinces before federal obligations are met, leaving the centre fiscally constrained.

2. Defence

Given border tensions and regional instability, defence expenditure is non-negotiable in the current environment.

3. Debt Servicing

Interest payments now consume the largest single share of revenue

Every new loan increase next year’s repayment burden

This is the core trap.

4. Federal Own Expenditure

A. PensionsGrowing faster than inflation and revenue growth.

B. Federal Development SpendingAlready compressed to dangerous levels, harming long-term growth.

C. Other Operational ExpensesNecessary but inefficiently structured.

D. State-Owned Enterprises Losses / Circular DebtChronic inefficiencies continue draining the treasury.

THE NET RESULT

Even after collecting more revenue:

Expenditure exceeds income

Fresh loans are taken

Next year’s debt servicing rises further

Debt servicing eats a larger share of revenue

This creates a permanent rollover economy — borrowing not for growth, but to survive.

This cycle cannot end without structural change.

WHY EXPENSE CUTS ALONE WILL FAIL

Federal expenditure is already peanut sized compared to obligations

Further cuts will collapse governance

Defence spending cannot be reduced under current geopolitical realities

The problem is not spending alone — it is how revenue is generated and distributed.

STRUCTURAL SOLUTIONS (NOT COSMETIC FIXES)

1. Revenue Must Increase by at Least 100%

Incremental growth will not work.Pakistan needs a one-time structural jump, not annual tinkering.

2. Bring at Least 50 million People Into the Tax Net

A. Employer-Based Tax Inclusion

Private industry, agriculture, SMEs, and households to increase minimum monthly salary threshold by PKR 100,000

This alone can bring 50 million earners into the documented economy

Industries will not suffer, as:

No super tax on businesses

Payment shifts from profit tax to employee income documentation

This is broadening, not burdening.

B. Currency Reform

Cancel all high-value currency notes

Retain only PKR 5 notes, primarily for beggars

This instantly kills:

Black money

Hoarding

Cash-based corruption

C. One Citizen, One Bank Account

Each individual allowed only one bank account

Fully traceable identity-linked system

D. Mandatory Banking Transactions

All transactions through banks

No exceptions for trade, property, or services

E. Super-Computerized Tax System

Automatic income and expenditure matching

No manual returns

No harassment

No discretion

Tax calculated by system, not officials.

F. No Cash Business

Cash economy must end

Every rupee must leave a digital footprint

3. NFC Award Must Be Revised

Provinces must share responsibility for:

Debt servicing

National obligations

Fiscal federalism without accountability is unsustainable

4. Federal Expense Cuts Will Not Save the System

The federal government is already operating on minimal flexibility.Cuts alone cannot handle the crisis.

5. Defence Budget Is Not the Villain

Given Pakistan’s security environment, defence spending cannot be compromised without existential risk.

FINAL WORD

Pakistan does not suffer from low revenue alone.It suffers from narrow taxation, cash addiction, and structural denial.

Until:

Everyone earns through banks

Everyone pays tax automatically

Every rupee is documented

The country will keep borrowing —not to grow, but to breathe.
