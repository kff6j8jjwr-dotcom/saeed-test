Pakistan’s Budget Trap: A Case for Structural Solutions

By Saeed Rasheed

Rising revenue has not prevented Pakistan’s debt from expanding because obligations outpace income. Interest payments, provincial transfers, pensions, development needs, and state-owned enterprise losses leave the federal government with little flexibility.

Why Cuts Are Not Enough

Federal operating expenditure is small compared with broader obligations. Cutting it further cannot resolve the deficit and may weaken governance. The underlying challenge is how revenue is generated, documented, and distributed.

Structural Reform

The article proposes a much broader tax net, greater use of traceable banking and digital transactions, automated taxation, and revision of fiscal responsibilities between the federation and provinces. Its central case is that Pakistan needs structural change, not cosmetic adjustments, to move from borrowing to survive toward sustainable growth.
