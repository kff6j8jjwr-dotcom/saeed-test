Iran–US Conflict: Strategic Realities, Future Risks & Pakistan’s Role

By Saeed Rasheed

BOARD OF PEACE AND THE IRAN–US CONFLICT: STRATEGIC REALITIES, FUTURE RISKS, AND PAKISTAN’S ROLE

The ongoing tensions between Iran and the United States continue to shape the geopolitical landscape of the Middle East and the broader Muslim world. While open full-scale war has not materialized, the current situation reflects a strategic pause rather than a permanent resolution. Both sides appear to be calculating their next moves carefully, balancing military, economic, and political considerations.

Strategic Delay and Economic Realities

Iran’s current posture suggests a deliberate strategy of delay. Direct military confrontation with the United States carries enormous financial and structural costs. Iran’s economy has already been under pressure from sanctions, currency depreciation, and reduced oil revenues. A prolonged war would impose further burdens on Iran’s infrastructure, defence budget, and internal stability.

By delaying escalation, Iran may be attempting to preserve its strategic assets while avoiding immediate large-scale destruction. This delay also provides time for diplomatic manoeuvring and internal consolidation.

United States Strategic Objectives

The United States’ long-term objectives appear to extend beyond immediate military considerations. Analysts widely believe that Washington seeks several commitments from Iran, including:

Limitation or termination of nuclear weapons development

Reduction of regional military influence

Compliance with international monitoring mechanisms

The United States also seeks assurances to protect its regional allies and maintain strategic balance in the Middle East. Any future negotiations may involve broader regional participation, including Muslim countries such as Pakistan, Saudi Arabia, and others.

Impact of Previous Military Actions

Recent years have seen targeted operations against Iran’s nuclear scientists and senior leadership associated with strategic programs. These actions significantly impacted Iran’s specialized command structure. However, Iran’s conventional military, including its regular army, remains structurally intact and capable of maintaining national defence and internal order.

This indicates that while Iran’s strategic leadership suffered losses, its institutional military capability continues to exist.

Internal Stability and Public Unrest

Internal unrest in Iran has also emerged as a factor influencing strategic calculations. Economic hardship, inflation, and political tensions have contributed to periodic protests. However, such internal developments are influenced by multiple factors, including domestic governance, economic pressure, and international sanctions.

The stability of Iran’s internal structure will play a critical role in determining its future strategic direction.

The Role of the Board of Peace

In this complex environment, institutions such as the Board of Peace have an important role to play in promoting dialogue, stability, and reconstruction. The Board of Peace can contribute to several key areas:

Promoting diplomatic engagement and conflict resolution

Supporting humanitarian and reconstruction initiatives

Facilitating cooperation among Muslim countries

Encouraging peaceful political transitions and stability

Pakistan’s Strategic and Economic Opportunity

Pakistan, due to its geographic position and diplomatic relations, is uniquely positioned to contribute to future reconstruction and peace efforts. Pakistani engineering firms, construction companies, and infrastructure specialists have extensive experience in post-conflict rebuilding and development projects.

Participation of Pakistani companies in reconstruction efforts could include:

Infrastructure rebuilding

Housing and urban development

Energy sector support

Technical and engineering services

Such involvement would strengthen Pakistan’s economic position while contributing to regional stability.

Outlook

The Iran–US situation remains fluid. Both sides are likely to continue using strategic pressure, diplomatic channels, and regional alliances to protect their interests. Direct war remains a high-risk option for both nations due to its unpredictable consequences.

The ultimate resolution will likely involve negotiation, international mediation, and regional cooperation rather than prolonged military conflict.

Conclusion

The current situation between Iran and the United States represents a critical moment in regional geopolitics. Strategic patience, diplomatic engagement, and international cooperation will determine whether the future brings escalation or stability.

Institutions like the Board of Peace and countries such as Pakistan have an opportunity to play constructive roles in promoting peace, stability, and reconstruction. The coming years will be decisive in shaping the future of the Middle East and its broader global implications.
