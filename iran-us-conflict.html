Iran–US Conflict: Strategic Realities, Future Risks & Pakistan’s Role

By Saeed Rasheed

The ongoing tensions between Iran and the United States continue to shape the geopolitical landscape of the Middle East and the broader Muslim world. While open full-scale war has not materialized, the current situation reflects a strategic pause rather than a permanent resolution. Both sides appear to be calculating their next moves carefully, balancing military, economic, and political considerations.

Strategic Delay and Economic Realities

Iran’s current posture suggests a deliberate strategy of delay. Direct military confrontation with the United States carries enormous financial and structural costs. Iran’s economy has already been under pressure from sanctions, currency depreciation, and reduced oil revenues. A prolonged war would impose further burdens on its infrastructure, defence budget, and internal stability.

By delaying escalation, Iran may be attempting to preserve its strategic assets while avoiding immediate large-scale destruction. This delay also provides time for diplomatic manoeuvring and internal consolidation.

United States Strategic Objectives

The United States’ long-term objectives appear to extend beyond immediate military considerations. Analysts widely believe that Washington seeks commitments from Iran including limitations on nuclear weapons development, reduced regional military influence, and compliance with international monitoring mechanisms. It also seeks assurances to protect regional allies and maintain strategic balance in the Middle East.

Impact of Previous Military Actions

Recent years have seen targeted operations against Iran’s nuclear scientists and senior leadership associated with strategic programs. These actions significantly affected Iran’s specialised command structure. However, Iran’s conventional military, including its regular army, remains structurally intact and capable of maintaining national defence and internal order.

Internal Stability and Public Unrest

Economic hardship, inflation, and political tensions have contributed to periodic protests. These internal developments are influenced by domestic governance, economic pressure, and international sanctions. The stability of Iran’s internal structure will play a critical role in determining its future strategic direction.

The Role of the Board of Peace

In this complex environment, institutions such as the Board of Peace can promote diplomatic engagement, humanitarian and reconstruction initiatives, cooperation among Muslim countries, and peaceful political transitions.

Pakistan’s Strategic and Economic Opportunity

Pakistan’s geographic position and diplomatic relations uniquely position it to contribute to reconstruction and peace efforts. Pakistani engineering firms, construction companies, and infrastructure specialists have extensive experience in post-conflict rebuilding. Participation could include infrastructure, housing, energy-sector support, and technical and engineering services—strengthening Pakistan’s economy while contributing to regional stability.

Outlook

The Iran–US situation remains fluid. Both sides are likely to continue using strategic pressure, diplomatic channels, and regional alliances to protect their interests. Direct war remains a high-risk option due to its unpredictable consequences. The ultimate resolution will likely involve negotiation, international mediation, and regional cooperation rather than prolonged military conflict.

Strategic patience, diplomatic engagement, and international cooperation will determine whether the future brings escalation or stability. Pakistan has an opportunity to play a constructive role in promoting peace, stability, and reconstruction.
